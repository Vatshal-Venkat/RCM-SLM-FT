# Medical Coding Basics for RCM

## Code sets used on claims

- ICD-10-CM: diagnosis codes describing why the service was performed. Updated every October 1.
- ICD-10-PCS: inpatient hospital procedure codes (institutional inpatient claims only).
- CPT (Current Procedural Terminology): five-digit procedure and service codes maintained by the American Medical Association, updated every January 1.
- HCPCS Level II: alphanumeric codes for supplies, drugs, durable medical equipment and services not in CPT, maintained by CMS.
- Modifiers: two-character codes appended to CPT/HCPCS codes to report that a service was altered (for example 25 significant separately identifiable E/M service, 59 or X{EPSU} distinct procedural service, 26 professional component, TC technical component).

## Medical necessity

Payers cover a service only when the diagnosis on the claim supports its medical necessity under coverage policy, such as Medicare National Coverage Determinations (NCDs) and Local Coverage Determinations (LCDs). A mismatch between diagnosis and procedure leads to denials such as CARC 50 or CARC 11.

## NCCI edits and MUEs

The CMS National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI) procedure-to-procedure edits identify code pairs that should not be billed together for the same patient on the same date unless an appropriate modifier applies; bundled services are denied (CARC 97). Medically Unlikely Edits (MUEs) set the maximum units of service normally reported for a code per patient per day.

## Coding accuracy and the revenue cycle

Coding errors cause denials, underpayments and compliance risk (overcoding can be treated as fraud; undercoding loses revenue). Coding accuracy targets are commonly 95% or higher, verified through regular coding audits.
