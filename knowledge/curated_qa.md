# Curated RCM Q&A

## RCM terminology

### What does ERA stand for in healthcare Revenue Cycle Management?

ERA stands for Electronic Remittance Advice. It is an electronic communication from a payer that provides claim payment, adjustment, and remittance information to a healthcare provider.

### What is an ERA in RCM?

An ERA, or Electronic Remittance Advice, is an electronic payer communication containing information about how submitted claims were processed, including payment and adjustment information.

### Explain ERA in simple terms.

An ERA, or Electronic Remittance Advice, is an electronic message from a health insurance payer explaining payment and adjustment information for submitted claims. RCM teams use it for payment posting and reconciliation.

### What information does an ERA provide?

An ERA can provide information about payer payments, adjustments, claim processing outcomes, and other remittance details needed for payment posting and reconciliation.

### How is an ERA used in payment posting?

An ERA provides payer payment and adjustment information that the RCM team can use to post payments to the appropriate claims, reconcile expected and received amounts, and identify discrepancies requiring follow-up.

### Why is ERA important in revenue cycle management?

ERA is important because it provides electronic payer remittance information that supports payment posting, reconciliation, account updating, and follow-up on claim payment discrepancies.

### A provider receives an ERA after claim adjudication. What should the RCM team do with it?

The RCM team should review the ERA's payment and adjustment information, post the appropriate payment, reconcile the remittance against expected reimbursement, and identify claims requiring follow-up.

### Is ERA related to payment posting?

Yes. An Electronic Remittance Advice provides payer payment and adjustment information that can support automated or manual payment posting and reconciliation.

### What does Electronic Remittance Advice mean?

Electronic Remittance Advice is an electronic payer communication that describes claim payment and adjustment information and supports provider-side remittance processing.

### What does EOB stand for in healthcare?

EOB stands for Explanation of Benefits. It is a statement that explains how an insurance claim was processed and describes applicable payment, adjustments, and patient financial responsibility.

### What is an Explanation of Benefits?

An Explanation of Benefits, or EOB, is a statement explaining how a health insurance claim was processed and what amount may be the patient's financial responsibility.

### What is the purpose of an EOB?

An EOB explains the outcome of claim processing and helps communicate amounts paid, adjusted, or assigned as patient financial responsibility.

### What is Revenue Cycle Management?

Revenue Cycle Management, or RCM, is the set of administrative and financial processes used to manage the flow of healthcare revenue from patient registration and eligibility through claim submission, payment, denial management, and final account resolution.

### What are the major stages of the healthcare revenue cycle?

Major RCM stages commonly include patient registration, eligibility verification, authorization when required, charge capture and coding, claim submission, claim processing, payment posting, denial management, accounts receivable follow-up, and account resolution.

### Why is accurate claim submission important in RCM?

Accurate claim submission helps reduce avoidable processing problems, rejections, and denials and supports timely and appropriate reimbursement.

### What is accounts receivable in RCM?

Accounts receivable represents money that is still owed to a healthcare organization for services that have already been provided and billed.

### What is accounts receivable aging?

Accounts receivable aging classifies outstanding receivables according to how long they have remained unpaid. Common aging buckets may include current, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days, and older categories. RCM teams use aging to prioritize follow-up and identify collection risk.

### What is a claim denial?

A claim denial occurs when a payer processes a submitted claim but determines that some or all of the billed amount will not be reimbursed under the applicable payer rules. Denials may require investigation, correction, appeal, or other follow-up depending on the reason.

## Comparisons

### What is the difference between an ERA and an EOB?

An ERA, or Electronic Remittance Advice, is an electronic payer remittance transaction primarily used in provider and RCM workflows for payment and adjustment information. An EOB, or Explanation of Benefits, is generally a statement explaining how a claim was processed and what financial responsibility applies, commonly provided to the patient.

### Are ERA and EOB the same thing?

No. An ERA and an EOB both communicate information about claim processing, but they serve different workflows. An ERA is an electronic payer remittance transaction used primarily for provider payment and reconciliation, while an EOB explains claim processing and financial responsibility, commonly for the patient.

### Differentiate ERA and EOB in simple terms.

ERA means Electronic Remittance Advice and is primarily used by providers and RCM systems for payment and adjustment information. EOB means Explanation of Benefits and generally explains claim processing and financial responsibility to the patient.

### Which is more directly associated with electronic payment posting: ERA or EOB?

ERA. An Electronic Remittance Advice provides electronic payer payment and adjustment information that can directly support payment posting and reconciliation workflows.

### A user says that ERA and EOB are identical. Is that correct?

No. They are related but serve different purposes. An ERA communicates payer remittance, payment, and adjustment information for provider-side workflows, while an EOB explains claim processing and financial responsibility, commonly to the patient.

### What is the difference between a claim rejection and a claim denial?

A rejection generally occurs when a claim fails an initial validation or submission requirement and is not accepted for adjudication. A denial generally occurs after payer processing when reimbursement is determined to be not payable in whole or in part.

### What should an RCM team do differently for a rejection and a denial?

For a rejection, the team generally focuses on correcting the submission problem so the claim can be accepted. For a denial, the team identifies the payer's adjudication reason and determines whether correction, resubmission, appeal, or another follow-up action is appropriate.

### Does a rejection usually occur before or after adjudication?

A rejection generally occurs before or during initial claim acceptance and prevents the claim from proceeding normally to payer adjudication. A denial generally occurs after payer processing.

### What is prior authorization?

Prior authorization is a payer requirement in which advance approval may be required before a particular healthcare service, procedure, medication, or item is provided or covered.

### What is the role of prior authorization in RCM?

Prior authorization helps determine whether payer approval is required before a service or item is provided. Missing required authorization can contribute to claim processing problems or denials.

### What is the difference between eligibility and prior authorization?

Eligibility verification checks whether insurance coverage is active and provides available benefit information. Prior authorization determines whether advance payer approval is required for a particular service or item.

The team should compare the remittance information with the expected reimbursement and investigate the difference using the claim, contract or reimbursement terms when available, and adjustment information. ### Differentiate denial management and accounts receivable follow-up.

Denial management focuses specifically on identifying, analyzing, correcting, appealing, and preventing claim denials. Accounts receivable follow-up is broader and focuses on resolving outstanding balances and receivables, which may include denied claims as well as other unpaid accounts.

### Compare rejection and denial in RCM.

Rejections are typically addressed by correcting submission or validation problems so the claim can be accepted and processed. Denials require understanding the payer's adjudication reason and may involve correction, resubmission, appeal, or other follow-up.

## RCM processes

### What is payment posting in RCM?

Payment posting is the process of recording payer and patient payments, adjustments, and related remittance information against the appropriate patient accounts and claims.

### Why is payment posting important?

Payment posting is important because it updates accounts with received payments and adjustments, supports accurate account balances, enables reconciliation, and helps identify discrepancies requiring follow-up.

### How does an ERA support payment posting?

An ERA provides electronic payer payment and adjustment information that can be used to apply payments and adjustments to the appropriate claims and reconcile received amounts.

### What happens during payment posting?

During payment posting, the RCM team records received payments and adjustments against the appropriate accounts or claims and reconciles the remittance information with expected reimbursement.

### Why is charge capture important in the revenue cycle?

Charge capture records the healthcare services provided so that appropriate charges and billing information can be included in claims. Incomplete charge capture can result in missed or inaccurate billing.

### How can inaccurate medical coding affect RCM?

Inaccurate coding can contribute to claim processing problems, payment delays, and inaccurate reimbursement. RCM teams should therefore support accurate coding based on the available documentation and applicable coding requirements.

### Why is claim scrubbing important?

Claim scrubbing is important because it can identify missing or inconsistent information before a claim is submitted. Finding problems earlier can reduce avoidable rejections and payment delays.

### Why is claim scrubbing important before claim submission?

Claim scrubbing can identify missing or inconsistent information before submission. Detecting these problems earlier can reduce avoidable rejections and payment delays.

### Why is coding accuracy important in RCM?

Coding accuracy is important because incorrect diagnosis or procedure coding can contribute to claim processing problems, payment delays, and inaccurate reimbursement.

### What information should be included in a claim denial summary?

A denial summary can include the claim identifier, payer, denial reason or code, service information, billed and allowed amounts when available, payment information, relevant dates, and the recommended follow-up action. The exact fields depend on the available claim and remittance data.

### Why is denial trend analysis important?

Denial trend analysis helps identify recurring problems that affect reimbursement. By grouping denials by reason, payer, provider, service, or other relevant dimensions, RCM teams can prioritize corrective actions and prevention strategies.

### How should an RCM team respond differently to a rejection and a denial?

For a rejection, the team generally focuses on correcting the submission problem so the claim can be accepted. For a denial, the team first identifies the payer's adjudication reason and then determines whether correction, resubmission, appeal, or another follow-up action is appropriate.

### Why is accurate payment posting important?

Accurate payment posting helps maintain correct account balances, reconcile payments against claims, identify underpayments or discrepancies, and support appropriate follow-up on outstanding amounts.

### Why should eligibility and authorization be treated as separate RCM checks?

They answer different questions. Eligibility verification checks whether coverage is active and provides benefit information, while authorization determines whether advance payer approval is required for a particular service or item.

### Why should payment posting be reconciled with remittance information?

Reconciliation helps ensure that payments and adjustments recorded in the system correspond to the payer's remittance information. It can help identify discrepancies that require further investigation.

### What information can an EOB contain?

An EOB can contain claim processing information such as billed charges, allowed amounts, payer payments, adjustments, and patient responsibility. The exact information and format depend on the payer.

## RCM reasoning

### Why is denial management important?

Denial management is important because unresolved denials can delay or reduce reimbursement. An effective process identifies root causes, prioritizes recoverable claims, and works to prevent recurring denial patterns.

### What should an RCM team analyze when denials increase?

The team should analyze denial reasons, payer patterns, providers, services, coding or documentation issues, eligibility and authorization problems, and other operational factors that may contribute to recurring denials.

### An RCM analyst sees a high number of claim rejections. What should they investigate?

The analyst should investigate recurring submission and validation problems such as missing or invalid claim information, formatting issues, coding-related edits, eligibility information, or other payer-specific acceptance requirements. The exact cause should be determined from rejection data rather than assumed.

### What should an RCM team check during eligibility verification?

The team should verify whether coverage is active and review relevant coverage and benefit information available from the payer. The exact information available depends on the payer and the verification process.

### A provider received an ERA after claim adjudication. What should the RCM team use it for?

The RCM team can use the ERA to review payer payment and adjustment information, post the payment to the appropriate account, reconcile expected and received amounts, and identify claims that require follow-up.

### What should an RCM model do when the available claim data is insufficient to determine the cause of a problem?

The model should clearly state that the available information is insufficient, identify what can be concluded from the available data, and specify what additional information would be needed. It should not invent payer decisions, denial reasons, coding errors, or reimbursement terms.
